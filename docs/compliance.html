Compliance Matrix

AAA-RNS — regulatory position, clause by clause

This page exists because the product's own thesis is that a claim without evidence cannot be recorded. Applying that rule to ourselves means publishing what AAA-RNS does not do as carefully as what it does. The word "compliant" does not appear as a verdict anywhere on this page, and never will. Compliance is a property of a validated, configured, documented and operated system inside your organisation. It is not a property of software, and no vendor can sell it to you.

Scope of this assessment. AAA-RNS is a local browser application. server.py binds to localhost only. There are no accounts, no cloud, no telemetry. The Content-Security-Policy is connect-src 'self', with one exception: three AI endpoints (Anthropic, Google, OpenAI), reachable only when the user explicitly opts in, off by default, and never required for any gate, seal, signature or verification. Team use is a company shared folder reached through the browser File System Access API. The source is published under Apache-2.0.

Not legal or regulatory advice. This is a supplier's self-assessment offered so that your QA, RDM or 연구관리 function can start its own review from a truthful baseline rather than from marketing copy.

Five structural facts that drive most of the verdicts

  1. There is exactly one identification component. A self-registered name and e-mail, plus a PIN stored locally as an unsalted SHA-256 hash. Not two components. Not a certificate. Not a federated identity.
  2. Time comes from the client machine's clock. There is no RFC-3161 code anywhere in the codebase, and no trusted time source of any kind.
  3. Integrity is strong and honestly bounded. A sealed note's SHA-256 covers both the content and the signatures (seal_algo: v2) and chains to the previous sealed note; verification recomputes the signature digest from the actual signatures rather than trusting a stored field. This makes alteration detectable. It does not make it impossible.
  4. There is no access control in the application. The PIN is a confirmation prompt, not an authorisation boundary. Real access control is the OS permission set on the shared folder, and it is yours.
  5. There is no validation package. No URS, FS or DS. No IQ, OQ or PQ. No traceability matrix. No validation summary report. Every clause that begins "the system shall be validated" is unmet at the supplier level.

1. US FDA — 21 CFR Part 11

The framing, stated plainly

The industry distinction is that a piece of software may be **Part 11-capable — it provides the technical controls the rule requires — while only a configured, validated, operated system, inside an organisation with the procedures Part 11 also demands, can be Part 11-*compliant***. That distinction is real, and it is routinely abused: vendors claim "capability" to imply "compliance."

We do not claim Part 11 capability either. As shipped, AAA-RNS fails § 11.200(a)(1) at the software level, by design, because it employs one identification component rather than two. Subpart C is therefore not merely unconfigured — it is architecturally unmet. Everything below follows from that.

Note also that FDA's 2003 guidance Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic Signatures — Scope and Application narrowed the rule's application and announced enforcement discretion for parts of it. That guidance changes what your inspector is likely to press on; it does not change what the regulation says, and this matrix assesses against the regulation.

§ 11.10 — Controls for closed systems

Whether your installation is a closed system at all is your determination, not ours: § 11.3(b)(4) defines it as "An environment in which system access is controlled by persons who are responsible for the content of electronic records that are on the system." Because access control here is the OS permission set on a shared folder, that condition is satisfied or defeated by your IT configuration. If the environment is open, § 11.30 additionally requires measures such as document encryption and appropriate digital signature standards — Not supported.

On Attributable

Attribution here has two separable properties, and honesty requires separating them.

What AAA-RNS can prove: that the attribution has not changed since the seal. Because seal_algo: v2 hashes the signatures together with the content, and verification recomputes the signature digest from the actual signatures rather than trusting a stored field, you cannot later swap a signer, add one, remove one, or move a signature between notes without breaking verification. That property is genuine, offline-checkable, and stronger than most electronic notebooks offer.

What AAA-RNS cannot prove: who the person was. The signer is a self-registered name and e-mail authenticated by an unsalted SHA-256 PIN hash on a folder your colleagues can read. There is no identity proofing, no certificate, no third-party attestation. A determined insider can sign as a colleague.

The precise claim is therefore: we can prove that the attribution has not been altered; we cannot prove the identity behind it. An auditor who accepts your institution's own identity procedures as the identity layer may accept this. One who requires the software to establish identity will not, and should not.

On Contemporaneous

The usual vendor answer to contemporaneity is a timestamp, and a timestamp from the client machine's clock is worth precisely as much as the operator's honesty — which is to say, exactly what this attribute exists to stop relying on. We will not dress that up.

What is genuinely unusual here is that AAA-RNS attacks contemporaneity from the other direction. Back-dating is rarely a matter of a wrong clock; it is a matter of reconstructing, weeks later, a narrative that fits the result you now know. G2 checks every new note for longitudinal consistency against every previously sealed note, so a reconstruction that contradicts the sealed history is caught and its finding retained. The chain fixes ordering irrespective of dates. Revisions append rather than overwrite. The effect is that writing a plausible retrospective record is expensive and leaves marks, even though writing a falsely dated one is trivial.

So: the content is made hard to back-fill; the clock is not made hard to move. Until a trusted timestamp exists, contemporaneity cannot be more than Partially supported, and any institution whose evidentiary needs turn on the time itself — patent priority in particular — should treat this as a blocking gap and not a soft one.

4. ISO/IEC 17025:2017 — § 7.5 Technical records, § 7.11 Control of data and information management

Citation caveat. ISO/IEC 17025:2017 is a copyrighted standard and is not freely republished. The requirements below are summarised, not quoted at length, and the sub-clause numbering within § 7.11 was cross-checked against secondary sources rather than a controlled copy. Verify the numbering against your own licensed copy before relying on it in an accreditation submission. Where our confidence in a sub-clause number is lower, we say so in the row.

§ 7.5 — Technical records

§ 7.5.1 is very nearly a restatement of this product's thesis, and it is worth being exact about how far that goes. The clause requires that technical records for each laboratory activity contain the results, the report, and sufficient information to facilitate — if possible — identification of the factors affecting the measurement result and its associated measurement uncertainty, and to enable the repetition of the laboratory activity under conditions as close as possible to the original. It further requires that records include the date and the identity of the personnel responsible for each activity and for checking data and results, and that original observations, data and calculations be recorded at the time they are made and be identifiable with the specific task.

"Enough information for a competent person not involved in the original work to repeat the activity" is, almost word for word, the standard AAA-RNS was built to enforce. But the correspondence is partial in a way that matters, and overstating it would be exactly the kind of claim this page exists to prevent:

  • G1 enforces that every narrative sentence maps to an evidence ID. That guarantees each claim has a source. It does not guarantee that the method description is complete enough to reproduce. A note can pass G1 while omitting a reagent lot, an instrument setting or an ambient condition, because those omissions are absences, and a gate that checks sourcing cannot detect an absence it was never told to expect.
  • G4 enforces required fields and attached originals. That covers the identity and date obligations and the "original observations attached" obligation.
  • Methodological sufficiency remains a scientific judgement made by the author and the reviewer. The product makes that judgement visible, reviewable and permanent. It does not make it automatic.

A note on 전자서명법 and the disappearance of 공인전자서명

The 전자서명법 was fully amended (전부개정) with effect from 10 December 2020, abolishing the 공인인증서 / 공인전자서명 regime after twenty-one years and giving all electronic signatures equal legal footing, with 전자서명인증사업자 and 운영기준 준수사실 인정 replacing the old 공인 designation.

Two consequences follow, and both cut against us:

  1. A demand for "공인전자서명" is now legally obsolete, so an institutional rule still phrased that way should be re-read against the current law before it is applied to any product.
  2. The abolition does not help AAA-RNS. The 지침 points at 전자서명법 제2조제5호 — 전자서명인증 — which still presupposes a third party confirming that the signing credential belongs to the signer. Equal legal footing among signature technologies does not turn a local PIN into a certified one. We will not describe our signature as 법적 효력이 있는 전자서명.

6. Japan — 厚生労働省 ER/ES指針 (薬食発第0401022号)

The three-pillar framing and the substance below are confirmed by multiple sources; individual sub-item numbering within 第3 and 第4 is not verified and is therefore not cited.

What we would need to build

In ascending order of cost. Each item names the clauses it would move and the honest reason it costs what it costs.

1. Explicit signature meaning, and a reason-for-change field — days

Add an enumerated meaning to every signature (authorship / review / approval / responsibility), include it in the signed payload and in the seal digest, and render it beside the name and time in every human-readable view and printout. Add a required reason-for-change when a revision is issued.

Moves: § 11.50(a)(3), § 11.50(b), Annex 11 § 9 (reason documented), 電子署名 署名の意味.

Why it's cheap: it is a schema field and a rendering change; the signing and sealing machinery already exists.

2. Salt and a memory-hard KDF for the PIN — days

Replace the unsalted SHA-256 PIN hash with per-user salt and Argon2id or scrypt.

Moves: nothing, formally. It creates no second factor and unlocks no clause.

Why it is nevertheless second on this list: a numeric PIN behind an unsalted SHA-256 on a shared folder is recoverable offline in seconds, and shipping that while publishing a page about evidentiary rigour is indefensible on its own terms.

3. Controlled human-readable rendition — 1–2 weeks

A deterministic print/PDF export carrying the full signature manifestation (name, time, meaning), the note's revision status, the evidence IDs behind every sentence, and the seal hash.

Moves: § 11.10(b), § 11.50(b), Annex 11 § 8.1 and § 8.2, 見読性.

4. Hash-chained, exportable audit log — 1–2 weeks

Chain the audit log the way sealed notes are chained, so that removing or reordering an entry is detectable, and export it in a generally intelligible form.

Moves: § 11.10(e) ("secure, computer-generated"), Annex 11 § 9 (available and convertible to an intelligible form), 真正性 監査証跡.

5. Trusted time — 2–4 weeks, plus a TSA relationship

An opt-in RFC-3161 timestamp token obtained at seal time and stored beside the seal for offline verification, and — for Korean institutions — 시점인증 through the 전자연구노트 포털 relay to the 행정안전부 전자문서진본확인센터.

Moves: § 11.10(e), § 11.50(a)(2), Annex 11 § 14(c), ALCOA+ Contemporaneous, 연구노트 지침 자동기록/시점인증, 真正性.

Why it costs more than the code: it requires a network call, which contradicts the product's offline guarantee. It must be per-note opt-in, must degrade silently to local time when offline, and must never become a precondition for sealing — otherwise the product stops working on an air-gapped machine, which is the reason several of our users chose it.

6. Certificate-based signature — 1–3 months, plus a CA relationship

Replace or augment the PIN with a non-extractable key pair (WebCrypto ECDSA held in IndexedDB) and, for jurisdictions that require it, a certificate from a Korean 전자서명인증사업자, an eIDAS trust service provider, or an equivalent.

Moves: § 11.100(a) and (b), § 11.200(a)(1)–(3), Annex 11 § 14, 연구노트 지침 제7조 전자서명인증, 電子署名法 本人性, ALCOA+ Attributable.

Why it costs what it does: the cryptography is the easy part. Key lifecycle is the hard part — enrolment, loss, revocation, device migration, and what happens to notes signed with a key that was later revoked. Every one of those is a product decision with an evidentiary consequence.

7. Per-user OS or OIDC identity, and real access control — months, and a product decision

Bind application identity to an OS account or an OIDC/SAML identity provider, enforce authorisation where it cannot be edited away by the record's own author, and retire the fiction that a client-side PIN is a control.

Moves: § 11.10(d) and (g), § 11.100(b), § 11.200, § 11.300(a)–(d), Annex 11 § 12.1 and § 12.3, ISO § 7.11.3.

Why it is the most expensive item that is still code: it ends "no accounts, no server, no identity provider," which is a founding property of the product and the reason it can run on a disconnected instrument PC. This is not a feature to be scheduled; it is a fork in the road, and it may be right to build it as a separate deployment mode rather than as an upgrade.

8. The validation package — the largest single line item

URS, FS and DS; a requirements traceability matrix carrying each requirement through design, test and release; IQ, OQ and PQ protocols with executed records; a validation summary report; and a supplier quality system capable of answering Annex 11 § 3.4 when an inspector asks.

Moves: § 11.10(a), Annex 11 Principle and § 3–4, ISO § 7.11.2 — that is, the clause that blocks first in every one of the three regimes above.

The honest structure of the work: most of it is document engineering plus a recorded, repeatable test harness — and it is only half the vendor's to do. A vendor can supply the specifications, the design evidence and the OQ protocols. The PQ is executed in your environment, against your process, by your people, and it is yours to own. Any vendor offering you a "pre-validated" system is offering you something that cannot exist.

9. Credential lifecycle — only meaningful after item 7

Password ageing, lockout, attempted-use detection and reporting, deauthorisation of compromised credentials, and an identity-verification workflow feeding § 11.100(b).

Moves: § 11.300(a)–(d), Annex 11 § 12.3.

Placed last deliberately: building password ageing on top of a single-factor client-side PIN would produce the appearance of a control without the substance, which is precisely the failure mode this page exists to avoid.
